Part A, Excel setup

The sign convention

  • Excel reads every amount as a cash flow with a sign.
  • Money you pay out is negative. Money you receive is positive.
  • PV, FV, and PMT return the amount on the other side of the deal, so the result carries the opposite sign.
  • A negative result means an outflow. Read the sign, do not delete it.

=FV(10%, 1, 0, -100)

You deposit $100 today (outflow, negative). Excel returns 110.00: the $110 you receive in one year.

=PV(10%, 1, 0, 110)

You want $110 in one year (inflow). Excel returns −100.00: the $100 you deposit today.

=PMT(10%, 5, 10000)

You borrow $10,000 (inflow). Excel returns −2,637.97: the payment you make each year.

Arguments

The arguments and two rules

ArgumentMeaning
rateInterest rate per period, i
nperNumber of periods, n
pmtUniform amount each period, A (0 if none)
pvPresent amount at time 0, P
fvFuture amount at time n, F
type0 or omitted: end of period (the textbook convention). 1: start of period

Rule 1: match rate and nper to the period

Monthly payments at 9% per year compounded monthly: rate = 9%/12, nper = years*12.

Rule 2: NPV starts at period 1

NPV discounts its first value one full period. Add the time-0 cash flow outside the function.

NPV in Excel: the time-0 rule

Excel's NPV(rate, value1, value2, ...) assumes the first value occurs at the end of period 1.

Engineering PW includes the time-0 investment, which needs no discounting.

Correct

=NPV(i, B3:B7) + B2
B2 holds year 0. B3:B7 hold years 1 to 5.

Wrong

=NPV(i, B2:B7)
Shifts every flow one year late. The result equals the correct PW divided by (1 + i).

Rate of return

IRR: the rate of return of a project

  • The internal rate of return is the interest rate that makes PW = 0.
  • By hand: trial rates, then linear interpolation between a positive and a negative PW.
  • In Excel: =IRR(B2:B7). Unlike NPV, the range includes year 0.
  • Optional guess: =IRR(B2:B7, 10%).
  • Decision: IRR ≥ MARR means accept. This matches PW ≥ 0 at the MARR.

0 = Σ CFt (1 + i*)−t

Solve for i*. For a project with one investment followed by returns, PW falls as i rises and crosses zero once.

NPV versus IRR ranges

=NPV(i, B3:B7) + B2 excludes year 0.
=IRR(B2:B7) includes year 0.

Summary

Six functions on one card

PV

=PV(rate, nper, pmt, [fv])

P from F or A. Replaces P/F, P/A.

FV

=FV(rate, nper, pmt, [pv])

F from P or A. Replaces F/P, F/A.

PMT

=PMT(rate, nper, pv, [fv])

A from P or F. Replaces A/P, A/F.

RATE

=RATE(nper, pmt, pv, [fv])

Unknown i from P, A, F, n.

NPV

=NPV(rate, yr1..yrn) + yr0

PW of an uneven series. Year 0 outside.

IRR

=IRR(yr0..yrn, [guess])

Rate where PW = 0. Year 0 inside.

Signs: out negative, in positive. Rate and nper: same period. Next week: annual worth and rate of return in depth.
